Three behaviours under one label: what an anchoring audit of a language model measures

With Herbert Chang · Preprint, September 2026 · Lightning talk at TADA 2026, UC Berkeley

We ask six instruction-tuned models (2B to 9B parameters) 506 numeric questions, with anchors set relative to each model's own unanchored answer, and read more than 200,000 answers. Every model anchors, and the smaller model in each family anchors more. A single anchoring index mixes three behaviours: agreeing with the direction word, copying the anchor, and comparing with under-adjustment.

Do the most active traders on a prediction market form a real core?

In preparation

On Polymarket, a small and densely connected group of traders does most of the trading. We test whether that group is more than a by-product of how much each trader trades, whether it profits at the expense of everyone else, and whether its trades move prices toward the correct outcome.

Is a rich core more than its degrees?

Manuscript and Python package, in preparation

Every network has a rich core, so finding one says little. This gives a test that compares the observed core with the one the degree sequence alone would produce, without generating random networks, and applies it to 24 public networks.

Activation steering for warmth and autonomy support in language models

With Freya Ma and Roshnik Rahat, Evergreen AI · June 2026 to present

A pre-specified comparison of steering vectors (CAA, RePS, BiPO) against DPO-LoRA and prompt optimisation in four open models, scored by two model judges that are checked against three human raters. In an earlier round, no method lifted judged warmth above the model's own baseline, and warm phrasing over cold content reproduced most of the judged gain.

Did the price move before the news? Information asymmetry on Polymarket

With Herbert Chang · Funded undergraduate research, March to June 2026

Across 500 Polymarket markets, we matched each price move to the first news report of the event (GDELT, ProQuest) to find windows where the price moved first. One case: an anonymous $400K bet placed at 5.5% hours before resolution, an 18.2x payout consistent with inside information.

A recall-first safety risk classifier for student journaling

Evergreen AI, mentor Michael Heinz · 2026

A small open-weight model (Ministral 14B) screens student messages for nine categories of acute risk and pages clinical staff on a flag. I built its 412-case gold standard set from public corpora and tuned the prompts for recall. Moving from three confidence tiers to a present/absent label removed 19 false positives.

Did California's 2018 board gender law (SB 826) affect the market value of California-headquartered public companies?

With Seohee Cho, Sophia Hinze, Julian Nelson and Eloise Riedel-Drouin · Econometrics, November 2026

A difference-in-differences comparison of about 1,300 listed firms in California and Texas, then the law as an instrument for board composition. We find no indication that the gender composition of a board affects market capitalisation.

How a system knows its own states, and how that affects its choices

Theories of Consciousness, November 2026

Two machines report "I am unsure about this answer." One reads its internal state; the other infers it from its own inputs and outputs. The paper asks whether both are monitoring themselves, and what turns on the answer.
